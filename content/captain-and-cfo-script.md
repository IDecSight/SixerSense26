## Part 1 — Think School (@ThinkSchool): what makes it work

**What it is:** an Indian business/geopolitics explainer channel co-founded by Ganeshprasad Sridharan ("Ganesh Prasad"), who presents on camera. It reportedly passed 3M subscribers within about two years of launch **[FC: current subscriber count]**. Its team describes a pipeline of scanning ~30 newspapers a day, choosing stories that have a business concept underneath, simplifying them into a narrative, and testing with experts and sample viewers before publishing.

### 1. Hook styles

| Hook type | How it works | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Paradox / contradiction** | Two facts that shouldn't both be true | "X has the best product in India, yet it's losing money every day. Why?" |
| **Shock number** | A big rupee/percentage figure in the first sentence | "₹___ crore. That's how much…" |
| **Headline montage** | Newspaper clippings and news anchors flash on screen, then the narrator cuts in | "Every paper is saying X. They're all missing the real story." |
| **Hidden genius / "real reason"** | Promises to show the strategy behind something familiar | "The REAL reason why…", "How X secretly…" |
| **Stakes for India** | Turns a company story into a national one | "…and this could decide India's next decade." |
| **Question title + answer withheld** | The title asks a question; the first 30 s raises it again and promises the answer at the end | Payoff saved for the conclusion |

### 2. Video structure (the usual template)

1. **Cold open hook (0–30 s):** a paradox, a number, or a headline montage. No introduction of the presenter first.
2. **Stakes + promise (30–60 s):** why this matters to *you* (founder, student, investor, Indian citizen), and a list of what the video will cover.
3. **Backstory ("to understand this, we need to go back to…"):** a short history with dates and an origin story.
4. **Core analysis in named chunks:** usually 3–5 "reasons" or "strategies", each with a framework label, a diagram, and a real company example.
5. **Twist / counterpoint ("but here's the problem…"):** the dark side, risks, or why the conventional reading is wrong.
6. **Lessons / framework recap:** the takeaways a founder or manager can use.
7. **CTA:** Think School courses or membership, a sponsor read, subscribe.

### 3. Recurring patterns

- **Framework naming:** abstract ideas get memorable labels (for example, the "crisis → hope" storytelling structure Ganesh teaches in his communication course).
- **Visual grammar:** dense motion graphics, highlighted newspaper clippings, maps, simple charts, stock B-roll with zoom/pan. This is why the format adapts well to **faceless** production.
- **Conversational, high-energy narration:** rhetorical questions every 20–40 s ("Think about it…", "Now this is where it gets interesting").
- **Indian lens:** global concepts explained with Indian examples (Reliance, Amul, Zomato, Blinkit, Old Monk, Milky Mist) and comparisons with China and the US.
- **Open loops:** a question raised early is answered late, which keeps people watching.
- **Data as punctuation:** a number lands at the end of each section to "prove" the point.
- **Moral close:** ends on a broader lesson (for nations, careers, founders), not just a summary.
- **Long-form default:** 10–20 min videos, plus Shorts cut from the strongest 30–60 s.

**What to borrow for this script:** a paradox cold open, named frameworks, "go back to…" storytelling, a counterpoint section, an Indian lens, and an open loop paid off at the end.

## Part 2 — Research brief: how captains and CFOs decide under uncertainty

| Decision principle | Captain case study | CFO equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Use information others don't have** | Dhoni promoted himself above Yuvraj in the 2011 WC final because Muralitharan was bowling and Dhoni had faced him a lot in CSK nets. Muralitharan later said Yuvraj "didn't have a clue" against him. | Proprietary data and "information edge" before committing capital (due diligence, channel checks) |
| **Put your best resources where leverage is highest** | Dhoni reportedly believed overs 17–19 matter more than the 20th, so he used his strike bowlers there and left the last over to Joginder Sharma (2007 WT20 final). | Capital allocation: fund the highest-marginal-return projects first and accept a weaker option where the stakes are lower |
| **Weigh asymmetric downside, not just the average** | Cummins bowled first in the 2023 WC final. Reports say he judged that getting it wrong while batting first would be fatal in a way bowling first wouldn't be, and he expected dew and better batting later. | Downside protection and asymmetric bets: avoid the "ruin" scenario even when it costs some expected upside |
| **Separate decision quality from outcome** | Joginder was hit for a six by Misbah; one mistimed scoop later, India won. The same plan could have lost. | Annie Duke's "resulting": judging a decision only by its outcome (Pete Carroll, Super Bowl XLIX) |
| **Reversible vs irreversible** | A bowling change is reversible next over. The toss in a final isn't. | Bezos's Type 1 (one-way door) vs Type 2 (two-way door) decisions, from his 2015 shareholder letter **[FC: letter year]** |
| **Imagine failure first** | Captains plan for the worst over before it happens | Gary Klein's "pre-mortem" (HBR, 2007) **[FC]** |

| VISUAL | VO |
|---|---|

## Part 4 — Fact-check checklist

1. **2007 final:** date (24 Sept 2007), venue (Wanderers, Johannesburg), India 157/5, Pakistan 152 all out, margin 5 runs.
2. **Joginder Sharma:** number of international appearances before the final; exact last-over sequence (wide, dot, six, then the scoop caught by Sreesanth).
3. **Harbhajan vs Misbah:** which over, and whether it was three sixes. (Secondary sources say Misbah hit him for three sixes in an over.)
4. **Dhoni's "overs 17–19 matter more" rationale:** sourced to teammate retellings (Sreesanth and RP Singh in media interviews). Present it as "reportedly", which the script does.
5. **Dhoni's age** in Sept 2007 (born 7 July 1981, so 26).
6. **2011 final:** target 275; score when Kohli fell; Dhoni's runs in the tournament before the final; 91\* off 79 balls; India won by 6 wickets.
7. **Murali quote** ("didn't have a clue"): confirm the original interview and wording.
8. **2023 final:** India's unbeaten run before the final; attendance; India 240 all out; Head 137; Australia won by 6 wickets; Cummins's quoted reasoning (SEN / ESPNcricinfo).
9. **Bezos Type 1 / Type 2 decisions:** confirm the shareholder-letter year (believed to be 2015, for fiscal 2015, published 2016).
10. **Gary Klein's pre-mortem:** HBR, September 2007, "Performing a Project Premortem".
11. **Pete Carroll, Super Bowl XLIX (Feb 2015):** the pass from the 1-yard line that Malcolm Butler intercepted; Duke's framing in *Thinking in Bets* (2018).
12. **Buffett's "circle of competence":** Berkshire shareholder letters (for example, 1996).
